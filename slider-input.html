Slider input

Slider input lets people pick a value from a range. Use it to capture a number, or to adjust settings such as volume and brightness. Changes are usually immediate, so people see the effect as they move along the track.

Usage

At its simplest, the slider can have a form label (label and helper text) and show the current, minimum and maximum values.

Product usage

  • Chronic Condition Management: choosing the low blood sugar reading that alerts a coach when a reading is at or below it
  • Video visits: adjusting the volume during a call with a provider

Variants and options

Available for iOS, Android, and web (mobile and desktop).

Placement

Desktop to mobile

  • The slider usually fills its content container horizontally.
  • Content containers rarely take the full width of the screen: side rails and centered containers keep components from stretching too wide.

Drawers and side rails

Like other form inputs, sliders can sit in side rails or flyout drawers, which is useful next to a video call. The slider fills the rail or drawer.

Interaction

Accessibility

Using a slider on a page still needs an annotated design review of the page-level design.

Screen readers

  • Every visible text must be announced, for single and double handles.
  • In code, each handle is a native range input, so screen readers hear its value, minimum and maximum. formatValue sets what they hear, such as "70 mg/dL".
  • Without a visible label, pass ariaLabel.

Keyboard

  • The left and right arrow keys move the handle along the track.

Code vs. Figma.
  • In code, the invisible native input gives the handle a 44 × 44px hit area on web too. Figma limits the web target to the 24 × 24px handle.
